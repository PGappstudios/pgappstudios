Let us answer the question directly, because it is the reason you are here. **No. Duolingo does not teach European Portuguese.** Its Portuguese course teaches Brazilian Portuguese, and only Brazilian Portuguese. If you are learning the language for Portugal, this is the single most important thing to understand before you sink weeks into the wrong material.

## The Straight Answer

Duolingo offers one Portuguese course for English speakers, and it is built around the Brazilian variety. That means the accent in the audio, the vocabulary choices, and several grammar patterns all reflect the Portuguese spoken in Brazil, not in Portugal. There is no toggle, no setting, and no separate European Portuguese track to switch to. What you get is Brazilian, full stop.

This is not a hidden fact or a criticism dressed up as news. It is simply how the course is designed, and Duolingo has never claimed otherwise. The confusion happens because the app just says "Portuguese," and beginners reasonably assume a language app would let them pick the variety.

## Why Duolingo Made This Choice

It is worth being fair here, because the decision makes business sense.

- **Audience size.** Brazil has a population many times larger than Portugal, and Brazilian Portuguese has vastly more speakers worldwide. Building for the larger market is a rational call.
- **Content demand.** The volume of learners interested in Brazilian Portuguese, driven by Brazil's cultural reach, dwarfs demand for the European variety.
- **Course maintenance.** Maintaining two full varieties of one language is expensive. Most large platforms pick the bigger one.

None of this is a knock on Duolingo. As a tool it is genuinely good at building daily habits, teaching core vocabulary, and keeping beginners motivated with its streaks and gentle gamification. The problem is not quality. It is fit.

## What This Means for Portugal-Bound Learners

If your goal is Portugal, learning from Brazilian material creates real friction that shows up later, when it is harder to unlearn.

- **The accent will not match.** European Portuguese reduces vowels and softens the final s into a sh sound. Train your ear on Brazilian audio and Lisbon speech will hit you like a wall.
- **Some vocabulary will be wrong.** You will learn "trem" for train instead of "comboio," "onibus" for bus instead of "autocarro," "banheiro" for bathroom instead of "casa de banho." In Portugal these can cause blank stares or genuine misunderstanding.
- **The grammar will steer you wrong.** Duolingo will teach you "estou fazendo" for I am doing, while Portugal uses "estou a fazer." It leans on "voce," while Portugal uses "tu" with its own conjugations you will never practice.
- **The citizenship test uses European Portuguese.** If you are on the path to Portuguese citizenship, the A2 language exam is based on the Portugal variety. Brazilian training leaves you underprepared for the exact thing you need.

To be clear, a learner is not ruined by starting with Duolingo. Plenty of vocabulary and structure overlaps, and the habit-building is valuable. But you will spend energy later correcting accent and word choices that a Portugal-specific course would have taught you right the first time.

## What to Use Instead

The good news is that European Portuguese resources do exist, even if they are less visible. Look for tools that explicitly say they teach the Portugal variety, then check the audio is by native Portuguese speakers. Options that take the European variety seriously include Practice Portuguese, Pimsleur's European Portuguese course, and Memrise when you select the Portugal variety.

## The Takeaway

Duolingo is a solid app, and if you are learning Portuguese for Brazil it is a perfectly good place to start. But for Portugal, it teaches the wrong variety, and no setting fixes that. Use it for habit and motivation if you like, but pair it with, or replace it with, a resource built for European Portuguese. Match your learning to your destination and the language starts working for you instead of against you.
