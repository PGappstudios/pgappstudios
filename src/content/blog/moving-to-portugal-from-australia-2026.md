Australians move to Portugal for many of the same reasons they love the Mediterranean: the coastline, the food, the pace, and a cost of living that compares well with Sydney or Melbourne. It also puts the rest of Europe a cheap flight away. What makes the move from Australia different is distance. Every document you forget is a courier, a time-zone gap and a few weeks away.

This guide is about doing it in the right order. Rules and thresholds change often, so check every detail with official sources (Portugal's diplomatic mission in Australia, AIMA, and Smartraveller for the Australian side) before you commit. Nothing here is legal, tax or financial advice.

## The 90-day rule, and why you need a visa

Australians can visit Portugal without a visa for **90 days in any 180** across the whole Schengen area. That clock covers every Schengen country together, so a month in Italy uses up a month of your Portugal time.

To live in Portugal you need a **national residence visa** before you travel, then a residence permit from **AIMA** after you arrive. Turning up as a tourist and "sorting it out later" is not a plan that works.

## Choosing a visa

- **D7, passive income.** For retirees and people with stable income that does not come from a Portuguese job: pensions, investments, rental income. The threshold is tied to the Portuguese minimum wage. Check the current figure when you apply.
- **D8, remote work.** For people who work remotely for employers or clients outside Portugal. The income requirement is much higher, around four times the Portuguese minimum wage.
- **D1 or D3** if you have a job offer in Portugal, **D2** to start a business or work independently there, **D4** to study.

Our [D7 vs D8 guide](/blog/portugal-d7-vs-d8-visa-guide) compares the two most common routes in detail.

## Documents: start these first

The documents that come from Australia are the slowest part, so begin with them.

- **Police check.** Portugal asks for a criminal record certificate. For Australians that usually means a national police check from the **Australian Federal Police**.
- **Apostille.** Australian public documents used in Portugal (police check, birth or marriage certificates) generally need an **apostille from DFAT**, and often a certified Portuguese translation.
- **Validity windows.** Police checks and some certificates are only accepted if recent. Time them so they are still valid on the day you apply.

Then the Portuguese side:

1. **Get a NIF**, Portugal's tax number, usually through a fiscal representative while you are still in Australia. Our [NIF guide](/blog/how-to-get-nif-portugal) shows how.
2. **Open a Portuguese bank account** for proof of funds and daily life.
3. **Arrange accommodation**, since the visa application needs proof of where you will live.
4. **Apply through Portugal's diplomatic mission in Australia.** Check whether your application goes to the embassy in Canberra directly or through a visa application centre.
5. **Arrive and attend your AIMA appointment** for the residence permit. Bring originals and copies of everything; our [AIMA appointment guide](/blog/aima-appointment-guide-portugal) has the list.

## A realistic timeline

| Stage | Rough timing |
|-------|--------------|
| AFP check, apostilles, translations | 1–2 months |
| NIF, bank account, accommodation | 1–2 months (in parallel) |
| Visa appointment and processing | 2–4 months, varies a lot |
| AIMA appointment after arrival | Weeks to many months |

Six months to a year from first document to residence card is common.

## Healthcare and money

- **Healthcare.** For the visa you will need private health or travel insurance. Once resident, you can register with the Portuguese health service (SNS) at your local health centre and get a *número de utente*. Many expats keep private cover as well. Remember that Medicare eligibility generally ends when you stop being an Australian resident.
- **Tax.** Spending more than 183 days a year in Portugal, or having your habitual home there, generally makes you Portuguese tax resident. Check how Australian income, investments and any pension are treated in both countries, and whether any agreement covers your situation. A cross-border adviser is worth the fee.
- **Super.** Moving overseas does not, on its own, let you access your superannuation early. Plan your Portugal budget around income you can actually use.
- **Banking.** Keep an Australian account open for anything tied to home, and use a low-fee transfer service for moving money.

## Where Australians tend to settle

Surfers head for Ericeira, Peniche and the west coast of the Algarve. Lisbon and Cascais suit people working remotely who want a city. Porto has a younger, creative scene, and the Silver Coast and Alentejo offer more space for the money. ## Citizenship and the language

Portugal lengthened its citizenship residence requirement in 2026 for many new arrivals, up from the previous five years, with transitional rules for people already resident. Check the final law for your arrival date. Citizenship also needs Portuguese at **A2 level**, usually through the CIPLE exam; see our [A2 test guide](/blog/a2-portuguese-citizenship-test-guide).

## FAQ

### Do Australians need a visa to live in Portugal?

Yes. You need a national residence visa such as the D7 or D8 before you travel, then a residence permit from AIMA.

### How long can Australians stay in Portugal as tourists?

90 days in any 180-day period across the whole Schengen area.

### What documents need an apostille?

Australian public documents used in Portugal, such as your AFP police check and birth or marriage certificates, generally need an apostille from DFAT.

### How long does the process take?

Six months to a year from first document to residence card is common.
