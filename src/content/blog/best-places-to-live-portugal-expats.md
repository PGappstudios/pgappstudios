Portugal keeps landing near the top of every "where should I move" list, and honestly, it earns the spot. Mild weather, a low crime rate, genuinely warm people, and a cost of living that still undercuts most of Western Europe. But "Portugal" isn't one place. Where you land shapes your whole day-to-day, from your rent to whether you hear English on the street or need to order your coffee in Portuguese.

Here's an honest look at the spots most expats consider, what each one actually feels like, and who fits where.

## Lisbon — the capital energy

Lisbon is the obvious starting point, and for good reason. It's a proper European capital with the airport, the jobs, the startup scene, and an international community big enough that you'll never feel like the only foreigner in the room.

The trade-off is cost. Lisbon is the most expensive city in the country by a clear margin, and rents in central neighbourhoods have climbed hard over the last few years. You get a lot for it though: hills with river views, world-class food, and that famous golden light everyone photographs.

**Who it suits:** remote workers, entrepreneurs, and anyone who wants city life with the ocean 20 minutes away. If you want quiet and cheap, look elsewhere.

## Porto — Lisbon's soulful cousin

Porto is smaller, grittier, and a lot of people quietly prefer it. The river Douro cuts through a postcard of a city, the port wine cellars sit right across the water, and the pace is calmer than the capital.

It's cheaper than Lisbon while still offering real city amenities and a growing tech presence. Winters are wetter and greyer than the south, so if constant sun is your dream, factor that in.

**Who it suits:** people who want authentic city living without Lisbon's price tag, and anyone who values character over polish.

## Cascais — the polished coastal option

Half an hour west of Lisbon by train, Cascais is where money goes to relax. Marina, beaches, palm-lined promenades, and a large, well-established international crowd.

It feels safe, tidy, and easy to settle into with English widely spoken. It's also pricey, and some find it a little too manicured. But if you want beach-town calm with quick access to a capital, it's hard to beat.

**Who it suits:** families, retirees, and higher-budget expats who want convenience and coastline.

## The Algarve — sun, golf and the retiree classic

The southern coast is the stereotype for a reason. Over 300 days of sun a year, stunning beaches, golf courses, and towns like Lagos, Tavira and Faro that have hosted foreign residents for decades.

Summer is busy and touristy, winter empties out and some spots feel sleepy. The eastern Algarve is quieter and more traditional; the central strip around Albufeira is louder and more built-up. Costs vary wildly depending on how close you are to the beach.

**Who it suits:** retirees, sun-chasers, and anyone who wants an established English-speaking community by the sea.

## Braga — young, affordable and rising

Up in the north, Braga is one of Portugal's oldest cities and also one of its youngest in spirit, thanks to a big university population. It's noticeably cheaper than Lisbon or Porto, cleaner and greener than you'd expect, and increasingly on the radar for tech and remote workers.

You'll need more Portuguese here, since the international scene is smaller. That's a feature, not a bug, if real immersion is what you're after.

**Who it suits:** budget-conscious movers, younger expats, and people who want to actually integrate rather than live in a bubble.

## Coimbra — history, students and calm

Coimbra sits between Lisbon and Porto and is best known for its ancient university, one of the oldest in Europe. It's a mid-sized city with a strong student energy, beautiful old streets, and a relaxed rhythm.

Costs are moderate, the community is smaller, and life moves gently. It's not a beach town and not a nightlife capital, but for a quieter, culture-rich base it's lovely.

**Who it suits:** academics, families, and anyone wanting an authentic small-city Portugal without going fully rural.

## Madeira — the island escape

Madeira is a floating garden in the Atlantic, closer to Africa than mainland Portugal. Funchal, the capital, has become a genuine hotspot for remote workers thanks to its digital nomad village and spring-like weather year-round.

The landscape is jaw-dropping: mountains, levada trails, and dramatic coastlines. The catch is that it's an island, so flights add up and options are more limited than on the mainland.

**Who it suits:** nature lovers, digital nomads, and people who want mild weather every single month.

There's no single "best" place, only the best place for you.
