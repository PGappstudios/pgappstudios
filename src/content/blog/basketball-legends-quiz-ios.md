Every basketball fan has a friend who can name a player from the jersey number, the college and one playoff run. **Basketball Legends** is for proving you're that friend. It is a trivia and "guess the player" game covering decades of the sport, from classic NBA icons to modern superstars.

## How it plays

There are two kinds of challenge, and you switch between them as you go.

### Guess the Player

Identify a player from a photo, a stat line, the teams they played for or what they achieved. Some are instant (it's hard to miss Jordan, Kobe, LeBron or Curry). Others are built to stump even serious fans.

### Trivia Challenges

Hundreds of questions across basketball history: championship moments, MVP seasons, records and the games people still talk about. It covers more than the NBA too, with questions that touch on the EuroLeague, college basketball and international tournaments.

## Levels that get harder as you do

You advance through stages that increase in difficulty, unlocking new question sets as you improve. Early rounds warm you up with the famous names; later ones go deep into eras, role players and records.

Every question teaches you something, so even a wrong answer adds to your basketball knowledge for the next round.

## Compete and play anywhere

- **Leaderboards** let you compare scores with friends and players worldwide.
- **Offline play** means it works on a plane, a train or anywhere without signal.
- **Quick rounds** fit into a short break, or chain them together for a longer session.

## How to get better at basketball trivia

1. **Learn the eras.** Group players by decade (the Russell and Chamberlain years, the Magic and Bird 80s, the Jordan 90s, the Kobe and Duncan 2000s, and today's game). Knowing the era narrows down any guess.
2. **Know the records.** Many questions come back to a handful of big records: Wilt's 100-point game, Bill Russell's eleven rings, Kobe's 81 points, LeBron passing Kareem.
3. **Remember nicknames.** "The Mailman", "The Dream", "The Answer". Nicknames show up a lot.
4. **Read the clue carefully.** Teams, colleges and numbers in a clue usually point to one player once you think about the timeline.
