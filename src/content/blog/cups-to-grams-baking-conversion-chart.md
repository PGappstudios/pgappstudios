American recipes measure in cups. British and Australian recipes mostly weigh in grams. And a cup of flour weighs about 120 grams while a cup of sugar weighs about 200, because a cup measures **volume**, not weight. That is why so many cakes go wrong when a recipe crosses an ocean.

This page gives you the numbers in one place: a cups-to-grams chart for common baking ingredients, the difference between cup sizes, and oven temperatures in Fahrenheit, Celsius and gas marks.

## First: which cup?

There is more than one "cup":

- **US cup:** about **237 ml** (236.6 ml). This is the cup in almost every American recipe.
- **Metric cup:** **250 ml**. Used in Australia, New Zealand and many modern UK, Canadian and South African recipes.
- **Old imperial cup:** about 284 ml. You will only meet it in old British recipes.

The difference between a US and a metric cup is about 5%, which rarely ruins a stew but can change a cake. The chart below uses **US cups**; for a metric cup, add about 5%.

Spoons differ too: a tablespoon is **15 ml** in the US, UK and most of the world, but **20 ml in Australia**. A teaspoon is 5 ml everywhere.

## Cups to grams chart (US cup)

These are typical weights for ingredients spooned into the cup and levelled off, not packed or scooped. Different brands and methods vary slightly.

| Ingredient | 1 cup | ½ cup | ¼ cup |
|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose / plain flour | 120 g | 60 g | 30 g |
| Bread flour | 127 g | 64 g | 32 g |
| Granulated / caster sugar | 200 g | 100 g | 50 g |
| Brown sugar (packed) | 220 g | 110 g | 55 g |
| Powdered / icing sugar | 120 g | 60 g | 30 g |
| Butter | 227 g | 113 g | 57 g |
| Rolled oats | 90 g | 45 g | 23 g |
| Cocoa powder | 85 g | 43 g | 21 g |
| Chocolate chips | 170 g | 85 g | 43 g |
| Honey or maple syrup | 340 g | 170 g | 85 g |
| Milk | 240 g | 120 g | 60 g |
| Water | 237 g | 118 g | 59 g |
| Vegetable oil | 218 g | 109 g | 55 g |
| Uncooked white rice | 190 g | 95 g | 48 g |

**Butter shortcut:** one US stick of butter is ½ cup, 8 tablespoons or **113 g**.

## Why weighing beats cups

The same cup of flour can weigh anywhere from about 110 g to 150 g depending on whether you spoon it in or scoop it straight from the bag. Scooping packs it down, and too much flour is the most common reason for dry, dense cakes.

If you only change one habit, make it this: **weigh flour and sugar with a cheap kitchen scale.** If you must use cups, spoon the flour in loosely and level it with the back of a knife.

## Oven temperatures

| Fahrenheit | Celsius | Fan oven | Gas mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300°F | 150°C | 130°C | 2 |
| 325°F | 160°C | 140°C | 3 |
| 350°F | 180°C | 160°C | 4 |
| 375°F | 190°C | 170°C | 5 |
| 400°F | 200°C | 180°C | 6 |
| 425°F | 220°C | 200°C | 7 |
| 450°F | 230°C | 210°C | 8 |

Fan (convection) ovens cook hotter, so recipes written for a conventional oven usually drop the temperature by about **20°C** for a fan oven. Ovens vary a lot, so an inexpensive oven thermometer is worth having.

## Quick answers

- **1 cup of flour in grams:** about 120 g.
- **1 cup of sugar in grams:** about 200 g.
- **1 cup of butter in grams:** 227 g, or two US sticks.
- **350°F in Celsius:** about 175 to 180°C, or 160°C in a fan oven.

## FAQ

### How many grams is 1 cup of flour?

About 120 grams for all-purpose flour, spooned and levelled. Scooping straight from the bag can add 20 grams or more.

### Is a US cup the same as an Australian cup?

No. A US cup is about 237 ml and an Australian (metric) cup is 250 ml, roughly 5% more. Australian tablespoons are also bigger: 20 ml instead of 15 ml.

### How many grams of butter are in a cup?

227 grams, which is two US sticks of butter. One stick is 113 grams.

### What is 350°F in Celsius?

About 175 to 180°C in a conventional oven, or around 160°C in a fan oven.

### Why do my cakes come out dry when I use cups?

Usually too much flour. Scooping packs it into the cup. Weigh it, or spoon it in loosely and level it off.
