A breakup doesn't just hurt, it disorganises your whole life. Sleep, appetite, concentration, even how your phone feels in your hand all change overnight. Most advice is either "just move on" or a list of thirty things to do, neither of which helps when you can barely get through the morning.

This is a slower plan. One month, broken into weeks, with small things to do each day. You don't have to do it perfectly. You just have to keep showing up for yourself, a little, every day.

## Before anything: what's normal

In the first weeks it is normal to:

- Feel physically unwell: tight chest, poor sleep, no appetite or constant snacking.
- Replay conversations and rewrite endings that will never happen.
- Swing between relief, anger, sadness and hope in a single afternoon.
- Feel worse in the evenings and at night.

None of that means you are failing at getting over it. It means you are going through it.

## Week 1: Survive

The only goal this week is to get through each day with your basic needs met.

- **Sleep:** keep the same wake-up time, even after a bad night. Phone out of reach at night.
- **Food:** keep easy food in the house (bread, eggs, fruit, soup). Eat something three times a day, even if it is small.
- **Move:** a ten-minute walk outside each day. Daylight and movement both steady your mood.
- **Tell two people:** pick two friends or family members you can message when it gets hard. You don't need to explain everything.

**Each day:** write down one sentence about how you feel. Just one.

## Week 2: Create distance

This is when **no contact** does its work. It isn't a game or a strategy to win someone back. It is how a wound gets the chance to close: every message, profile check or "just checking in" reopens it.

- **Mute, unfollow or archive.** You don't have to block or announce anything. You just remove the daily reminders.
- **Move the photos** into a folder you don't see. You can decide later what to keep.
- **If you share children, a home or work,** keep contact to logistics only: short, polite, no history, no feelings.
- **When you want to text them,** write it somewhere else instead. Read it the next morning. Most of these messages are better unsent.

**Each day:** count one day of no contact. If you slip, start again tomorrow. It's a habit, not an exam.

## Week 3: Rebuild small routines

By now there will be days that feel slightly lighter, and others that feel like day one. Both are normal; healing is not a straight line.

- **Bring back one thing that's yours.** A sport, a class, a hobby, a weekly coffee with a friend. Small and repeatable beats big and exhausting.
- **Plan your weekends.** Unstructured time is when it hurts most. Put two small plans in each weekend.
- **Notice your thinking.** "I'll never meet anyone" is a feeling, not a fact. Write the thought down, then write one sentence that is more balanced.

**Each day:** one small, doable step. Make your bed, call someone, cook a proper meal, walk somewhere new.

## Week 4: Make meaning

This is not about being "over it". It is about starting to understand it.

- **Write an unsent letter.** Say everything you never said. You can keep it, or let it go: burn it safely, or delete it with intention.
- **Look at the pattern, gently.** What did you need that you didn't get? What would you want differently next time?
- **Look back at week one.** Compare your notes. Most people are further along than they feel.

**Each day:** write one thing you are grateful for that has nothing to do with them.

## The 2 a.m. moments

Hard moments arrive without warning. Have a plan before they do:

- **Breathe slowly:** in for four, hold for seven, out for eight. Repeat four times.
- **Change the room or the light.** Get up, drink water, sit somewhere else.
- **Write instead of sending.** The urge passes faster than it feels like it will.
- **Message one of your two people,** or a helpline if it is very bad. That is what they are there for.

## When to get more support

Talk to a doctor or a counsellor if, after several weeks, you still can't sleep or eat properly, can't work or look after yourself, are drinking more to cope, or feel hopeless. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, contact a crisis line or emergency services right away. Asking for help is part of getting through this, not a sign it has gone wrong.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to get over a breakup?

There is no fixed timeline. Many people notice the worst of it easing after a few weeks to a few months, with good and bad days along the way. Steady routines and no contact usually help it pass faster.

### Does no contact really work?

For most people, yes. It isn't about winning anyone back; it removes the constant reminders that keep reopening the hurt, so it can start to heal.

### Is it normal to feel physically sick after a breakup?

Yes. Poor sleep, appetite changes and a tight chest are common in the first weeks. If they don't ease or you can't function, talk to a doctor.

### What should I do when I want to text my ex?

Write the message somewhere else and read it the next morning. Most urges pass within minutes, and you can decide with a clear head.

### When should I see a professional?

If after several weeks you still can't sleep, eat, work or look after yourself, or you feel hopeless, talk to a doctor or counsellor. If you have thoughts of self-harm, contact a crisis line immediately.
