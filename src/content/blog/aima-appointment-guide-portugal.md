If you are moving to Portugal, one acronym will dominate your paperwork life: **AIMA**. It is the agency that decides whether you get your residence permit — and, thanks to a major backlog, it is also the part of the process people find most stressful. Understanding what AIMA is, what its appointments are for, and how to prepare can make a genuinely frustrating experience far more manageable.

One important note up front: immigration processes in Portugal change frequently, and the situation with AIMA in particular has been evolving. Treat this as a general guide and always verify the current process through official AIMA channels. This is not legal advice.

## What is AIMA?

**AIMA** stands for **Agência para a Integração, Migrações e Asilo** — the Agency for Integration, Migration and Asylum. It was created to replace the former immigration authority, **SEF** (Serviço de Estrangeiros e Fronteiras), which was dissolved.

In practice, AIMA now handles most of what SEF used to do for immigrants: processing residence permits, renewals, family reunification, and integration matters. If you hear long-time expats still saying "SEF," they are referring to the old name for essentially the same function.

## What AIMA appointments are for

An AIMA appointment is where the human, in-person part of your residency process happens. Depending on your situation, an appointment might be for:

- **Finalising a residence permit** after arriving on a visa (like the D7 or D8)
- **Renewing** an existing residence permit
- **Family reunification** — bringing a spouse or children
- **Biometrics** — fingerprints and photo for your permit card
- **Submitting or verifying documents** connected to your application

The appointment is typically where they check your documents, take biometrics, and move your file toward the issuing of a physical residence card.

## The backlog reality

Here is the honest part. When SEF was dissolved and AIMA took over, an enormous backlog of pending cases built up — hundreds of thousands of applications. This has meant:

- Long waits to even get an appointment
- Special task forces and mission structures set up to clear the queue
- Extensions of document validity so people are not left without legal status while they wait
- Frequent changes to how appointments are scheduled and handled

The situation has been improving in phases, but the key expectation to set is **patience**. Delays are common and largely outside your control. What you *can* control is being organised, responsive, and ready the moment your appointment comes.

## How to prepare your documents

The single best thing you can do is have every document ready, complete, and correctly organised. A missing paper can mean rescheduling — and another long wait. General tips:

- **Bring originals and copies.** Do not assume they will photocopy for you.
- **Keep documents current.** Background checks, proof of income, and insurance often have validity windows — make sure nothing has expired.
- **Match your visa type.** A D7 file and a D8 file need different income evidence; bring what your specific route requires.
- **Have your NIF and proof of address ready.** These come up constantly.
- **Bring proof of accommodation.** A rental contract or property documents.
- **Print your appointment confirmation.** Have the date, time, and reference easily accessible.
- **Arrive early.** Give yourself buffer time to find the office and check in.

A simple checklist you tick off the night before saves a lot of morning panic.

## Key vocabulary you'll hear

Even though some staff speak English, appointments are conducted largely in Portuguese, and understanding the key words reduces stress enormously. A starter list:

| Portuguese | Meaning |
|------------|---------|
| Autorização de residência | Residence permit |
| Marcação | Appointment |
| Documentos | Documents |
| Comprovativo | Proof / supporting document |
| Morada | Address |
| Renovação | Renewal |
| Reagrupamento familiar | Family reunification |
| Cartão | Card |
| Prazo | Deadline / time limit |
| Assinatura | Signature |

Being able to recognise "comprovativo de morada" (proof of address) or "autorização de residência" when an official says it means you follow the conversation instead of freezing.

## Final thoughts

AIMA is the gatekeeper of your legal status in Portugal, and yes, the backlog has made it a source of real frustration. But the people who navigate it best are not the lucky ones — they are the prepared ones. Verify the current process through official AIMA channels, keep your documents complete and current, learn the key Portuguese vocabulary, and go in organised and calm. The system is slow, but it does move — and preparation is how you make sure that when your turn comes, you are ready.

## FAQ

### What is AIMA?

AIMA is Portugal's agency for immigration and asylum, which replaced SEF in 2023. It handles residence permits and renewals.

### What should I bring to my AIMA appointment?

Originals and copies of everything: passport, visa, proof of address, proof of income, health insurance and any documents listed in your appointment notice.

### Why are AIMA appointments so hard to get?

Demand has far outstripped capacity, so waits can be long. Prepare your documents early and check for new slots regularly.
